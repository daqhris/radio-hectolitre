◂ The performance

Sound, Electric,
Windy Performance

A site-specific performance unfolding inside the Reactor space at Hectolitre. Musicians, scattered through the architecture, move like unstable signals — appearing and disappearing between resonant corners while a "mother board" redirects and contaminates their sounds.

Feedback stretches across the rooms, whispers dissolve into fragile tones and circulate through hidden frequencies. The space itself answers back, transforming into a living surface of resonance and interference.

Broadcast live via FRP Radio — the inaugural transmission of Radio Hectolitre. The garden was open as a chill-out area after the performance. Documentation photography by Mladen Bundalo. Poster design by Bel McLaughlin.

◂ Words spoken in the space
Chris-Armel Iradukunda (daqhris) — voiced during the performance
"Time Board Mother Control has booked a timeslot for a collective toast to an artists-run space where residents watered with sweat a dry ground until growing inter-national wings and flying information in formation towards Italy's 61st Venice Biennale."
Refaat Al-Areer — "If I Must Die, Let It Be a Tale" — read aloud in the space
During the performance, daqhris read aloud "If I Must Die, Let It Be a Tale" by Refaat Al-Areer (1979–2023), Palestinian poet and professor, killed in an Israeli airstrike in Gaza in December 2023. The poem was written days before his death and circulated across the world as both elegy and demand.
Two artists — one alive, one no longer — both physically absent from the world's most attended art fair and presently exhibiting their works. The poem was not background. It was a reason to be in the room.
